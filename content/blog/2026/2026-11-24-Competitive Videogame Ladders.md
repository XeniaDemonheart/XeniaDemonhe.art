{{ <indent n={2} /> }}<span style="color: #a6e3a1;">I've</span> been busying myself lately with grinding ranked ladder in a couple hyper competitive games. Hearthstone and Sonic Crossworlds specifically. They are similar but on separate sides for climbs. One is purely strategic/RNG, one is twitch reflex intensive with some track and item memorization. And Hearthstone is very hard to climb (usually), Sonic Crossworlds is extremely easy to climb to top rank.

{{ <youtube id="VYWUolK8n2E" /> }}

Reason why Sonic Crossworlds is easy to climb is that, at least where I'm at for now + very near top rank, they give you points even if you don't place in places 1-3 at the finish line. Very kid-friendly, nothing against it really, just not a super serious ranking system.

{{ <indent n={2} /> }}Games like these and others have a ranked system that tries to sort people into matches of similar skill level, depending on the time and skill the individual is willing to put in and how many wins or losses they accrue over a temporary period of time. A bell curve ELO system.

[![Alt text](https://files.catbox.moe/xk6rzw.png)](https://files.catbox.moe/xk6rzw.png "Hearthstone ranked mode, going from Bronze to Legend rank of six ranks")

{{ <indent n={2} /> }}The ranks are similar across many competitive games. Bronze, silver, gold, platinum, diamond, legend/top rank/grand master. A vast majority of players will find themselves "stuck" in gold or silver rank, unless they truly improve in skill/are willing to put serious time in. And can physically manage to beat other hyper competitive opponents. Crossworlds uses a F to S tier list, alphabet letters replacing the Words, like you're being graded on a report card when you reach A+ rank.

[![Alt text](https://files.catbox.moe/1ukram.jpeg)](https://files.catbox.moe/1ukram.jpeg "Street Fighter 6's ranked mode and how many players are at each rank, going from nearly 100% of percentile in Rookie to 13% percentile in Master Rank")

{{ <indent n={2} /> }}I've always wanted to reach top rank in a videogame... I don't exactly need top 100 or top 1000 status, and I don't Need to win tournaments. Literally just getting into the category would be enough for me. I'm not really doing it for glory, there is no applause, no glory waiting for you in top 100. Especially someone who isn't even in an esports team like myself. I'm not doing it for cash prize money. But I enjoy being competitive in games, I enjoy dominating human opponents. I have for quite a while, in lots of games. I had a spin on Paladins for a while. I enjoy being completionist in games sometimes, doing 100% of content, getting achievements.

[![Alt text](https://files.catbox.moe/1vwom0.png)](https://files.catbox.moe/1vwom0.png "Xenia Demonheart's own Steam Achievements data from her public profile page, showing 100% completion on Elden Ring and 16 100% completed games total")

{{ <indent n={2} /> }}And I think my brain sees getting into a top rank as The Real Last Completion Step to beat 100% of a game. Sometimes, it literally is. Paladins has a steam achievement to get into top rank. It is one of two Steam achievements I never got in that game, of a 58 set, of 650 hours of play.

[![Alt text](https://files.catbox.moe/jaq742.png)](https://files.catbox.moe/jaq742.png "Xenia's Paladins progress, showing 650 hours of play and a few never unlocked achievements, including a Reach Master in Ranked achievement")

{{ <indent n={2} /> }}Once upon a time, years ago, I tried this same grind and climb in Brawlhalla, a platform fighter like Super Smash Bros. I was very unhealthy and very driven during that period, I gave myself carpal tunnel, or some similar nerve pain hand condition. Playing for hours matches over and over even when it started hurting.

[![Alt text](https://files.catbox.moe/0bsdhg.png)](https://files.catbox.moe/0bsdhg.png "Some unlocked achievements in Brawlhalla, spaced over a week in 2017")

{{ <indent n={2} /> }}I never got top rank in that game. I got within a stone's throw, I was about to graduate and needed to only win 3-5 more matches. And then I lost. And again. And over and over. And the pain became unbearable. I considered my hand physically disabled for 1-3 years after that climb. I had wrist pain daily. I messed myself up hard. It was stupid. My wrist is, healed-ish? There's still something Wrong with it, but it's not outright pain.

{{ <youtube id="FF5K0WvqhBE" /> }}

Either that or I got used to the pain and it no longer registers as pain.

{{ <indent n={2} /> }}I can tell I'm being somewhat unhealthy in my Hearthstone play. I'm playing for hours and hours, barely getting minimum calories in a day, not being terribly great about bathing/hygiene. I'll be fine, don't worry. I'm better now about doing self maintenance than I was in my teens. Waaay better medicated, too. But still, I can tell I could be more multi-faceted in what I'm doing in a day, or not playing *As* much of this game as I am currently.

{{ <indent n={2} /> }}Right now in Hearthstone, a digital card game akin to Magic or Yugioh somewhat, there's a few top competitive meta decks. There always are in card games, there's always a Near Best Deck that will always be hyper viable to win 60% of the time with. And then new cards come out, or cards get nerfed/rebalanced, and the process starts all over again. A few of those hyper viable decks right now are Aura Paladin, Cannon/Pirate Warrior, Quest Priest, Elemental Shaman.

[![Alt text](https://files.catbox.moe/g6jp8n.png)](https://files.catbox.moe/g6jp8n.png "A screenshot of a Hearthstone tier list showing the top-performing archetypes.")

{{ <youtube id="YBqVX0A1gg4" /> }}

{{ <indent n={2} /> }}I'm running Cannon Warrior, which does have the highest winrate right now. The deck is absurdly powerful, annoying, problematic. I can tell, though there is no direct chat, that people get extremely furious at the matches and my deck. It doesn't bother me terribly. In card games especially, in competitive modes, it's hard to have much sympathy. This deck is pretty easy to build. Hearthstone actually is letting you outright buy it right now mostly complete, for 5 USD. It costs a LOT of money to play physical card games like Yugioh and Magic, this is nothing. And to boot, some of the best decks right now had a week at the beginning of current expansion where you could get the entire deck for free. 

{{ <indent n={2} /> }}Anyone can play this same deck. Anyone can use the same cards I'm using. They choose not to since a lot of people don't see using the top competitive deck as fun or interesting, when a lot of the fun of card games is deck and player expression. My own creativity is I will nibble and take out and put in cards from a meta deck, but it will still overall be a meta deck in the end because I want to win on ladder.

{{ <indent n={2} /> }}And, besides being on even playing field, I value my fun and happiness over my opponent's unhappiness. If they get mad or furious over a loss, that's their problem. It's easily something you could bring to a therapy session, dealing with losses, anger, frustration in competive games. Honestly I probably need that therapy too, when I'm not just winning I deal with losses as badly as most everyone else does.

{{ <indent n={2} /> }}Sooo, point? Is there any Real point to doooing this? No, not really. The funny numbers make my brain feel good, the wins and flashing lights of a victory screen give my brain dopamine, and that's really all I need to be enjoying myself and seeing my own Point in spending the time and effort to do this. It's a little milestone to reach that is relatively meaningless compared to the thousands of hours I've spent playing and beating games and opponents in general. I enjoy competition for competition's sake.

{{ <indent n={2} /> }}There is a general consensus in the gaming world population that Ranked and Ranks have at least a bit of meaning on player skill. We've chosen to use ELO, ranks, as a general catch-all descriptor to sum up skill in a game. Saying "I'm gold in Overwatch" or "I'm top 100 Overwatch" gives people a very quick overview on hours and effort you put into a game. There is some small or large amount of respect given if you truly distinguish yourself as able to be the best of the best in a hyper competitive game that a great many people know of or play casually.

{{ <indent n={2} /> }}In the end, it's about having fun in whatever way your own personal fun is had. There are people who find competition, Player VS Player, to be nauseating. I find it intoxicating. I find it addictive. I find it enjoyable. Feeling very Spike as I write all this, a bit edgy. (Gee whiz I wonder if goth girl Xenia Demonheart likes being edgy sometimes...)

[![Alt text](https://files.catbox.moe/hj4s5b.png)](https://files.catbox.moe/hj4s5b.png "A reddit comment explaining the Magic the Gathering Archetypes of Timmy, Johnny, Spike")

But I really, really do love putting an opponent's nose in the dirt.

{{ <indent n={2} /> }}I am 33 years old as of writing. There will come a time very soon, if not already, that I will no longer physically be able to be top level competitive in an action twitchy game like Unreal Tournament, Fortnite, or Sonic Crossworlds. That time might already be now really, I feel my actions and brain have already noticeably slowed down VS when I was 20. 

I will still be able to play strategy only games like Hearthstone or Mahjong for the foreseeable future, until extreme old age.

And then once even that goes away...

Well, you <span style="color: #fab387;">know.</span>
